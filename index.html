Lesson 1: Risk modeling with extreme value theory

Most of a return distribution is irrelevant to tail risk. Extreme value theory (EVT) models only the far tail, using a family of distributions that the theory says the tail must belong to, so it can extrapolate to losses larger than anything yet observed.

Two approaches

MethodData usedDistribution
Block maximaThe largest loss in each block (month, year)Generalized extreme value (GEV)
Peaks over thresholdEvery loss above a high threshold uGeneralized Pareto (GPD)

Both share a tail index ξ. If ξ > 0 the tail is heavy and decays like a power law, which is typical of financial losses. If ξ = 0 it decays exponentially, and if ξ < 0 losses have a finite upper bound. For the Student-t distribution with ν degrees of freedom, ξ = 1/ν.

With a fitted GPD, VaRp = u + (β/ξ)[((n/Nu)(1 − p))−ξ − 1] and ESp = VaRp/(1 − ξ) + (β − ξu)/(1 − ξ), where n is the sample size and Nu the number of exceedances.

The estimated ξ usually sits below the true 1/ν at lower thresholds, because the body of the distribution still influences the fit. Raising the threshold reduces that bias but leaves fewer exceedances, so the estimate gets noisier.

Lesson 2: Block maxima, Indonesian gold

Gold quoted in rupiah, such as Antam bars, carries two risks at once: the world gold price and the USD/IDR exchange rate. Block maxima turns a long daily history into a handful of worst-loss observations and fits a GEV distribution to them.

Procedure

  1. Compute daily losses (negative returns) and split them into blocks.
  2. Keep the worst loss in each block.
  3. Fit a GEV with location μ, scale σ and shape ξ.
  4. Read off return levels: the loss expected to be exceeded once every T years, xT = μ + (σ/ξ)[(−ln(1 − 1/T))−ξ − 1] with T counted in blocks.

The block size is a trade-off. Short blocks give many maxima, but they may not be extreme enough for the theory to apply. Long blocks fit the theory better, but leave few observations and noisy estimates.

Lesson 3: The theorem and politics of extreme values

The Fisher–Tippett–Gnedenko theorem is to maxima what the central limit theorem is to averages: whatever the underlying distribution, properly normalized block maxima can only converge to one of three shapes.

The theorem

LimitShape ξTailParents
Fréchetξ > 0Heavy, power-lawStudent-t, Pareto
Gumbelξ = 0ExponentialNormal, exponential, lognormal
Weibullξ < 0BoundedUniform, beta

The companion Pickands–Balkema–de Haan theorem says that losses above a high threshold follow a generalized Pareto distribution. Together, they justify both methods from Lesson 1.

The politics

Emil Julius Gumbel, whose name the Gumbel distribution carries, wrote the field’s classic text, Statistics of Extremes (1958). Earlier, in Weimar Germany, he documented political murders committed by the far right and was forced out of his post at Heidelberg in 1932. He left Germany in 1933, and the Nazi government stripped him of citizenship the next year. His story is a reminder that statistics of extremes concerns real events.

Tail modeling is political in a quieter sense too. Choosing a tail model, a confidence level, or VaR versus ES decides how much capital banks hold and who bears the cost of rare events. Before the 2008 crisis, many risk models understated tails, and regulation later moved toward expected shortfall in the Basel market-risk framework. Different tail assumptions give very different answers, which is why they get contested.

Lesson 4: Extreme innovations in VaR modeling

Plain EVT assumes returns are independent, but real volatility clusters. The conditional EVT approach of McNeil and Frey filters out the volatility first, then fits the tail to what is left.

Conditional EVT in two steps

  1. Estimate today’s volatility σt with GARCH or EWMA, and form standardized residuals z = r/σ.
  2. Fit a GPD to the tail of the residuals and set VaRt = σt × qp(z).

Volatility moves immediately, and the EVT quantile handles the tail shape. Other innovations build on this: time-varying thresholds, the Hill estimator for ξ, multivariate EVT for joint extremes, and hybrids with machine learning. In every case, the confidence level, threshold and window matter, so test them out of sample.
